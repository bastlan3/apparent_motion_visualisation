Farrell & Shepard (1981) — shape, orientation & apparent rotational motion

Faithful reconstruction of Farrell, J. E. & Shepard, R. N. (1981), J. Exp. Psychol.: HPP 7(2), 477–486. A polygon (18 radii; ≈ 4.8° visual angle) is generated at a chosen approximation to 180° rotational symmetry (0/25/50/75/100%) and shown in continuous alternation between an upright orientation and one rotated by 30–180° in the picture plane — each view immediately replacing the other (no interstimulus interval). Starting at SOA = 1 s, the observer speeds up in 10% steps until rigid rotation breaks down (SOA₁), then slows until it re-emerges (SOA₂); critical SOA = their mean. For asymmetric polygons the critical SOA rises ~linearly with disparity (~2 ms/°); for near-/fully-symmetric polygons it peaks near 90° and falls (an inverted-V), because beyond 90° a shorter supplementary rotation in the opposite direction competes — rigid rotation through the larger angle becomes unstable. The smooth rigid rotation is the observer's percept; only the two real alternating views are drawn.
