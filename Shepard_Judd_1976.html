Shepard & Judd (1976) — apparent rotation of 3-D objects

Two views of one object alternate in the same place, with no gap. Watch for as long as you like, then rate the quality of the apparent rotation.

Display setup
Resize the bar until it is as wide as a credit card (8.56 cm):
Instructions. Two views of one object will alternate in the same place. Watch for as long as you need, then rate the quality of the apparent rotation on a scale from 1 to 3, using tenths between the points if you wish:
1 — strikingly like seeing an actual 3-D object rocking back and forth; the object seems to rotate as a rigid whole throughout its entire trajectory.
2 — apparent movement, but nonrigid or noncoherent: for large angles, different parts appear to move independently or to deform into other non-corresponding parts (jumbling); for small angles, a vibration; or the object moves too rapidly to be followed as a rigid whole.
3 — the two alternating views appear superimposed; flickering but no apparent movement.
Refresh rate is re-measured when the session starts.

Methods (Shepard & Judd, 1976, Science 191, 952–954)

ParameterPaperThis page
StimulusPerspective line drawings of Shepard–Metzler ten-cube objects, each view in a circular field; end cube nearest the top coloured yellow in both conditions (p. 953, Fig. 1)Computer-drawn ten-cube objects (one depth object, one picture-plane object), black edges on white inside a circle, top end cube yellow, hidden faces removed. Assumption: the exact Shepard–Metzler objects, orientations, size and viewing geometry are not given in the article; these are look-alikes. Circle diameter 10 deg (assumed; whether the circle is a physical aperture is not stated); object scaled so its farthest point from the circle centre is about 0.62 of the circle radius (measured on Fig. 1 rendered at 500 dpi: the object spans about 0.55 of the diameter in height and 0.6 in width), one scale per object for all views; yellow #f2d200.
ConditionsRotation in depth about the object's vertical axis, or in the picture plane (a different object for each condition); angular differences 20, 60, 100, 140, 180° (p. 953, first paragraph)Same levels. Assumptions: absolute start orientations (views placed symmetrically about a base view); the picture-plane pair is drawn as a pure rotation of the image about the circle centre (Fig. 1A may also involve a perspective change); which view of a pair is shown first is randomised on each trial.
PairsPer angle: 1 picture-plane pair and 2 depth pairs of the single depth object (two different pairs of perspective views, so that no one hard-to-interpret pair dominates) (p. 953)Same: P (picture-plane object), D1 and D2 (two pairs of views of the one depth object). Assumption: D1 and D2 differ in base orientation (yaw 25 deg / tilt −22 deg vs yaw −40 deg / tilt +18 deg).
PresentationTwo fields of an Iconix tachistoscope in continuous alternation; one field ends as the other begins (no ISI); equal field durations (p. 953)Single canvas, views swapped on a count of display frames; no blank between fields; alternation continues until the rating is submitted (assumed). A browser is not a tachistoscope: display latency cannot be controlled, and dropped frames are counted and logged.
Field durationsNine rates, 500 to 40 ms; the nine values are not listed (p. 953, 954)
ResponseRating 1–3 with tenths allowed; observers trained on the scale beforehand; unlimited time; angle and rate not announced (p. 953–954)Click-anywhere scale 1.0–3.0 in 0.1 steps (no preset position) or typed value, with the paper's three anchors; angle and duration hidden. Instruction screen shows the anchors. Assumptions: on-screen scale as response device; 5 unlogged practice trials (the number is not given).
Trial structure10 observers; 5 angles × 3 pairs × 9 rates = 135 ratings each. Three equal blocks by condition, depth pairs half before and half after the picture-plane block. Each pair rated at all nine rates before moving to the next angle; angle order counterbalanced across observers; rate order randomised within each angle (p. 954)Blocks D1 / P / D2 (45 trials each; assumed: D1/D2 order swaps for even observers). Angle order is a cyclic rotation of 20-60-100-140-180 by observer number (assumed; same order in every block); the nine durations are shuffled for each angle.
Size, distance, luminance, fixationNot specifiedAssumptions: viewing distance 57 cm, 10 deg circle (see Display setup), black on white, no fixation mark.
