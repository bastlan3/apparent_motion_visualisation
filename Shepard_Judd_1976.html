Shepard & Judd (1976) — perceptual illusion of rotation of 3-D objects

Two perspective views of a ten-cube object alternate (no gap). Below a critical rate it looks like one object rocking rigidly; above it, motion becomes nonrigid, then mere flicker.
Shorten each field's duration until the rigid rocking breaks down. Rate what you see: 1 rigid rotation · 2 nonrigid/jumbling · 3 flicker, no motion.

Faithful reconstruction of Shepard, R. N. & Judd, S. A. (1976), Science 191, 952–954. Two perspective views of a Shepard–Metzler ten-cube object (top cube coloured yellow to disambiguate) alternate continuously in the same location — the termination of one field is the onset of the next (no interstimulus interval). The two views differ by an angular difference of 20/60/100/140/180°, either as a rotation in depth about the object's vertical axis or as a rotation in the picture plane. Observers rate the motion 1 (rigid) / 2 (nonrigid) / 3 (flicker); the breakdown of rigid rotation is the field duration where the rating crosses 1.5. That threshold rises linearly with angular difference at nearly the same slope for both conditions (picture-plane 1.10 ms/° + 121 ms; depth 0.97 ms/° + 112 ms). The smooth rigid rotation through intermediate orientations is the observer's percept — only the two real alternating views are drawn.
