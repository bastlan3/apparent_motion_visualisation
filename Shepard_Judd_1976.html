Shepard & Judd (1976) — apparent rotation of 3-D objects

Two views of one object alternate in the same place, with no gap. Watch for as long as you like, then rate the quality of the apparent rotation.
Before the session you can practise with the experimenter free-play controls below (not logged).
Rate the apparent rotation from 1 to 3; values between the points (tenths) are allowed.
—
1 — strikingly like seeing an actual 3-D object rocking back and forth; the object seems to rotate as a rigid whole throughout its entire trajectory.
2 — apparent movement, but nonrigid or noncoherent: parts move independently or deform (jumbling) for large angles, a vibration for small angles, or the motion is too rapid to follow as a rigid whole.
3 — the two views appear superimposed; flickering but no apparent movement.

Methods (Shepard & Judd, 1976, Science 191, 952–954)

ParameterPaperThis page
StimulusPerspective line drawings of Shepard–Metzler ten-cube objects, each view in a circular field; end cube nearest the top coloured yellow in both conditions (p. 953, Fig. 1)Computer-drawn ten-cube objects, black edges on white inside a circle, top end cube yellow. Assumption: the exact objects/views of the paper are not reproduced in the article; these are look-alikes.
ConditionsRotation in depth about the object's vertical axis, or in the picture plane (a different object for each condition); angular differences 20, 60, 100, 140, 180° (p. 953)Same levels. Assumption: absolute start orientations (views placed symmetrically about a fixed oblique view).
PairsPer angle: 1 picture-plane pair and 2 depth pairs (different depth objects/views) (p. 953)Same: pair P, D1, D2.
PresentationTwo fields of an Iconix tachistoscope in continuous alternation; one field ends as the other begins (no ISI); equal field durations (p. 953)Single canvas, views swapped on a frame count; no blank between fields; alternation continues until the rating is submitted.
Field durationsNine rates, 500 to 40 ms; the nine values are not listed (p. 953)
ResponseRating 1–3 with tenths allowed; observers trained on the scale beforehand; unlimited time; angle and rate not announced (p. 953–954)Slider 1.0–3.0 in 0.1 steps with the paper's three anchors; angle and duration hidden. Assumption: slider as response device; no practice trials specified.
Trial structure10 observers; 5 angles × 3 pairs × 9 rates = 135 ratings each. Three equal blocks by condition, depth pairs half before and half after the picture-plane block. Each pair rated at all nine rates before moving to the next angle; angle order counterbalanced across observers; rate order randomised within each angle (p. 954)Blocks D1 / P / D2 (45 trials each; assumed: D1/D2 order swaps for even observers). Angle order is a cyclic rotation of 20-60-100-140-180 by observer number (assumed; same order in every block); the nine durations are shuffled for each angle.
