Chatterjee, Freyd & Shiffrar (1996) — Experiments 1–5: two alternating pictures

Instructions (paraphrase of p. 919–920, not the original wording). You will see two pictures of a model in different poses flashing rapidly in alternation. You may sometimes see motion between them. Look for as long as you need to decide which path the moving part takes, then click Stop below the pictures. A “Please Respond” screen follows; click it, then say whether the path of motion was around or through. Click to start the next trial.
Display setup
Resize the bar (drag its right edge) until it matches the width of a credit card (8.56 cm).

Schematic stand-ins. The paper used full-colour video photographs of a human model, wooden boards/blocks and a wooden mannequin (Figs 1, 2, 4, 7, 8). The pictures here are flat drawings that reproduce only the layout: the body (or object) stays fixed and one limb (or board) appears at either side of a body part (or block). Pictures, poses, displacements and colours are assumptions. Responses are collected internally only and never shown.

Parameters (paper values)

DisplayMacintosh 21 in. colour display, 1152 × 870 px, 40 × 30 cm screen (p. 918)
Viewing distance80.5 cm, chin rest (p. 918–919). Each image 8 cm high × 10 cm wide = 5.68° × 7.08°. Here the picture is drawn at 7.08° × 5.68° from the pixels-per-cm and viewing distance set in Display setup.
FramesTwo pictures (A, B) of one pair alternate, each followed by a blank interval (p. 919)
SOA133, 266, 400, 533 ms; stimulus duration = ISI = SOA/2 = 66.5, 133, 200, 266.5 ms (p. 919). Converted to whole frames at the measured refresh rate (see the display line).
Presentation lengthOpen-ended: pictures flash repeatedly until the observer clicks a stop button on the monitor just below the pictures (p. 919)
ResponseAfter stop: blank screen with a "Please Respond" button, then a two-alternative choice, "around" or "through" (the inanimate object or the body), then a button to start the next trial; the experimenter starts each new block (p. 919–920)
Exp 116 observers. Body about body vs body about object; 7 pairs each: right foot about left foot; left foot about right ankle; right foot about left knee; right hand about face; right hand about left knee; right hand about waist; right arm about bent right knee (model seated). Object condition: matched box (or chair-backrest corner) of about the same 2D size/orientation, placed adjacent to and not occluding the body part it replaced, which stays visible (p. 918). Every pair at all 4 SOAs; 8 blocks of 7 trials (4 per condition) = 28 trials per condition; condition order counterbalanced; pairs and SOAs randomised within condition (p. 919).
Exp 216 new observers. Body about body (as Exp 1) vs object about body: a wooden board cut to the limb length, same position and orientation, appearing to float without a joint, replacing the limb; 7 pairs (left foot, left ankle, left knee, face, left knee side view, waist, right knee seated); 4 blocks × 7 trials per condition; same SOAs (p. 921).
Exp 316 new observers. Partial body: only the moving limb and the body part it moves about visible, remainder filled with black pixels; visible region 1.1° × 1.6° (as printed, taken as width × height). 7 stimuli × 4 SOAs in 2 blocks of 14 trials, random order (p. 923).
Exp 416 new observers. Object about object: wooden boards/blocks cut to the length of the displaced limb and of the stationary body part, same position and orientation, nothing else; Exp 3 parameters (p. 924–925).
Exp 516 new observers. Wooden artist's mannequin, enlarged to the same visual angle as the human model; same spatiotemporal parameters as Exp 3 (p. 925–926). Assumption: only the SOAs and the block structure are carried over; the 1.1° × 1.6° partial window is not applied, the full mannequin is shown (Fig. 8).
Practice4 picture pairs at 133 or 533 ms SOA, with inanimate objects or other models, different from test items (p. 919)

Not specified in the paper (assumptions here)

Backdrop and blank-interval colour (near-black used, like the dark backdrop of the published photographs; the Exp 3 surround is black as stated), fixation point (none), refresh rate (measured at load), which picture comes first (A), number of cycles before stop (unlimited), how the around/through report was given (the paper says only that observers “indicated” it, p. 920, so it may have been verbal; on-screen Around/Through buttons are used here, only the next-trial button is stated to be a mouse click), the drawn arm (a two-segment limb of constant length with a bent elbow; drawing stand-in only, the poses are given in words), button labels beyond "Please Respond" and stop, the prompt text "The path of motion was" and the labels "Around"/"Through"/"Next trial", instruction wording (the paper gives only its gist), left/right position of the Around and Through buttons (not specified; randomised per trial here), the stand-in shape drawn for the occluding body part in Exp 3 (a plain block at the part's position; the moving limb is drawn as in Exp 1), stimulus drawings and limb displacement (nominally ±0.40° of the occluder centre, i.e. 0.8° between the two pictures; widened per pair so the limb tip lies clearly beside the occluder, never on it: tip centre = occluder half-width + tip radius + 3 units; in Exp 3 the waist/other occluders are narrowed to at most 34 units so both limb positions fit in the 1.1° × 1.6° window; the paper only says the limb is clearly on either side of the part, p. 918), side views drawn as front views, practice stimuli (four invented layouts, not the paper's), how the 28 stimuli of Exp 3–5 are split between the 2 blocks (here: each pair gets 2 random SOAs per block) and how SOAs are spread over the 4 blocks of Exp 1–2 (here: each pair meets each SOA once per condition). Exp 1–2 session: both conditions in random order, 4 blocks each; Exp 3–5 session: 2 blocks of 14. Run session starts with the 4 practice trials.
