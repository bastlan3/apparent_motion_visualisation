Kolers & Pomerantz (1971) — Figural change in apparent motion

Two disparate shapes are flashed in sequence on a lit field while the observer fixates a point; the observer reports with button presses whether the transition looked smooth and continuous. Only the physical flashes are shown.

Methods (Kolers & Pomerantz, 1971, J. Exp. Psychol. 87, 99–108). Values from the paper; amber = not stated / my assumption.
Apparatus (p.99)Multichannel electronic tachistoscope; two shapes in sequence with two lighted pauses between. Target area 12.5 × 10.5 cm = 7° × 6° at 1 m viewing distance. Lamp rise 1.5 ms, 90% decay 3.5 ms. Field brightness ≈ 4 ft-L. Timing error < 1%.
Stimuli (p.100)Circle, square, triangle, hollow arrow drawn in black ink on white board, contour 1 mm wide (≈ 3.4′), area equated at 1.56π cm². A circle "subtended 43′" (this equals the circle's radius for 1.56π cm², so the page draws it at that area, diameter ≈ 86′). Centres of gravity coincide with Line A or B of Fig. 1. Shapes are outline only.
Layout (Fig. 1)Fixation point FP = small neon point source. Figure labels 3.7°, 1.7°, 0.5°. Positions here (B ≈ 1.2° from left edge, A ≈ 5.55°, FP ≈ 6.25°, all at mid-height) are scaled from the printed drawing; the dimension labels are ambiguous. FP colour/size are assumed (small orange dot).
Timing (Fig. 1, p.100)Shape 1 (25–400 ms; 80 ms in Exp I) → ISI 10, 25, 50, 71, 100, 125, 165, 210, 260, 390 ms (field lit) → shape 2 (same duration) → ICI 3200 ms during which the observer responds → next cycle. The browser's frame rate (~16.7 ms at 60 Hz) quantises shape and ISI durations; page timing is not tachistoscope-accurate, especially for the 10 ms ISI.
ViewingLight-adapted right eye only, fixation on FP. 3 min dark adaptation then 1 min light adaptation to a blank field before testing (not reproduced). Extensive practice before each session.
Exp I (pp.100–102)4 shapes → 16 pairs (4 same, 12 disparate); shapes 80 ms each; 10 ISIs; shapes centred on Lines A and B (assumed: first shape at A, second at B). Response: one button if smooth continuous motion was seen, another if anything else. Later part ("complement"): arrow→square, arrow→circle, arrow→triangle.
Exp II (p.104)Right-angled trapezoid (truncated isosceles right triangle), area equal to Exp I shapes, paired with its mirror reflection (M), inversion (I) or 180° planar rotation (R); both members centred on Line A. 5 durations (25, 50, 100, 200, 400 ms; the printed Fig. 4 legend) × 10 ISIs. Response during the ICI: button 1 smooth rotation of a rigid shape, button 2 smooth movement of a plastic shape, button 3 neither. Observers were first never told what to see, then given these instructions. Exact proportions of the trapezoid and which orientation is shown first are not given; I used legs L with the tip cut at 0.7 L, narrow end pointing left first.
Exp III (p.105)Five trapezoid pairs, each shape 100 ms: large left-pointing with its mirror image; small left-pointing with its mirror image; large left vs small left; large right vs small right; large left vs small right. Large = Exp II area, small = half that area. Superposed (both on Line A) and then separated (one on A, one on B). Response: one button for smooth continuous change of any kind, another if the transition was not smooth and continuous.
Not specifiedInter-trial order randomisation shown here is not used: the page repeats the selected condition. Which shape of Exp III pairs was first, shape-to-location mapping for the "separated" case, and the brightness of the shapes beyond "black ink on a lit board" are unspecified. Responses on this page are not recorded or scored.
