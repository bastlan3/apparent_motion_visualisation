Shepard & Zare (1983) — path-guided apparent motion

A faint gray curved band, flashed for 5 ms between two alternating dots, makes the dot appear to move along that curve. The minimum SOA scales with path length, not direct distance.
Fixate the centre. Lengthen the dot duration (10% steps) until you first see the dot move back and forth over the presented path (not just blink). Mark that point.

Faithful reconstruction of Shepard, R. N. & Zare, S. L. (1983), Science 220, 632–634. Two black dots (0.42°) alternate on a white ground; during the 5 ms interstimulus field a faint gray band (0.42° wide) traces a circular-arc path between them. Observers fixate the path centre and raise the dot duration in 10% steps (from 100 ms) until motion over the path first appears (SOA = duration + 5 ms field). Variable path: D fixed at 3°, arc length L = 3/4/6/9/12° → SOA ≈ 100 + 41·L. No-path control: blank field, dots 2⁄3–9° apart → SOA ≈ 170 + 32·D (Korte's law). Fixed path L = 6°, terminal distance D = 6/4/2/2⁄3/0° → SOA ≈ 166 + 540/D, rising sharply as the path closes into a circle (motion of the dot into itself; the closed circle adds 200 ms blank fields). The gray path is a real flashed stimulus and is drawn; the perceived moving dot is the observer's percept and is not.
