Faithful reconstruction of Experiment II of Attneave, F. & Block, G. (1973), Perception & Psychophysics 13(2), 301–307. Two 2-mm light apertures flash alternately (50 ms each) in a clear central alley; the surrounding grid is either a flat frontal grid (control) or a perspective grid depicting a plane receding ~70° into depth (experimental). The lights keep the same retinal separation (4 cm) in both — only the pictorial depth changes — yet the perspective display raises the minimum SOA for apparent movement, showing that Korte's law operates on phenomenal 3-D separation, not retinal distance. Threshold found by the ascending method of limits. Experiments I (real depth via a diagonal light array) and III (varied viewing distance) relied on physical 3-D apparatus that a flat screen cannot reproduce; this page reconstructs the inherently 2-D pictorial-depth experiment, which isolates the same phenomenon. Paper thresholds shown for reference.
