Experimenter parameters

Reconstruction of Ramachandran, V. S. & Anstis, S. M. (1985), Perception 14, 135–143. Only the physical dot flashes are drawn; nothing is recorded, scored or displayed about what is perceived. Original: P-4 phosphor CRT, Apple II, viewed at 1 m. Here the stimulus is drawn in degrees: pixels per degree follow from the Display setup (screen px/cm and viewing distance; default distance 100 cm = the paper's 1 m, 37.8 px/cm is an assumption until calibrated). The display refresh rate is measured at load; every duration is converted to a whole number of frames at that rate and scheduled by counting frames, and the realised durations are printed under the controls. Light dots on dark. A warning appears if part of a display falls outside the canvas at the current setup.

CycleTwo diagonal dots on, then switched off and replaced by the other two; repeated continuously (p.136). Only two dots visible at any instant.
SOAHand-held potentiometer, continuous 25–500 ms; SOA rather than ISI varied (p.136). Earlier experiments 100–200 ms (p.141); Exp 1 "3–4 frames/s" (p.137); Exp 5b: 200 ms within pairs, 300 ms between row A and row B (p.140); Exp 6: starts above 350 ms and is gradually lowered (p.141).
Exp 1 (p.136–138)Several squares simultaneously, side 1°, distance between squares 1.5° (centre-to-centre or edge-to-edge not stated; both offered, plus the pitch measured from Fig 3a), 3–4 frames/s; regular arrays (Fig 3a, 3 rows × 5 columns) and random positions (Fig 3b); 12 naive students. 3.1.1: two 3 × 5 clusters like Fig 3a, ~2° apart, one rotated exactly 45°, one per hemifield, 3 subjects. 3.1.2: dichoptic (crossed polaroids; upper two rows to one eye, third row to the other, binocular fixation cross between rows 2 and 3) — not reproducible here.
Exp 2 (p.138)Familiarised with drawings "percept 1/percept 2"; shown square; reports axis (vertical/horizontal); display on 30 s, switched off automatically, reappears after various random intervals; fixation cross on screen; told the axis might or might not have changed; 6 naive subjects. Realised: 30 s = 1800 frames.
Exp 3 (p.138–139)Square "random walk": after every 3–4 cycles switched off and reappears at a random screen location after about 500 ms (30 frames = 500 ms); fixation on a central cross; 6 naive subjects. (The head-tilt variant, eyes closed and head rotated 90° between presentations, 4 subjects × 10 trials, cannot be done on a page.)
Exp 4 (p.139, Fig 5)Single ambiguous square in the centre of an array of single dot pairs, left and right columns of 5 dot pairs, middle column with pairs in rows 1, 4, 5 and the square in rows 2–3; row pitch ≈ 1° and pair separation ≈ 1° (measured from Fig 5); every pair flashes left dot then right dot (horizontal excursions).
Exp 5a (p.139, Fig 6)3 cycles, then square replaced by a slightly rotated one, 3 more cycles, repeated in 20°–30° steps until a full 90° change; fixation cross in the middle of the square.
Exp 5b (p.140, Fig 7)Square embedded in a rectangular matrix of squares; column A switched off after a few cycles and replaced by horizontal row B; SOA 200 ms within pairs, 300 ms between A and B.
Exp 6 (p.140–141)Same four-dot display; 3 subjects asked to "will" clockwise/anticlockwise rotation, starting at SOA above 350 ms, while the SOA was gradually lowered.
Assumptions / unspecified in the paper: default SOA in Exp 2, 3, 4, 5a and basic display (200 ms); ISI taken as 0 (each frame lasts one SOA; flash duration, luminance, colour, background and screen size are not given); the small central mark drawn in Fig 2b/3a is not reproduced; SOAs are rounded to whole frames at the measured refresh rate (25 ms is not a whole number of frames at 60 Hz); square spacing in Exp 1: default is the pitch measured from Fig 3a (≈1.9° horizontally, gap 0.9°; ≈2.6° vertically, gap 1.5° as in the text), with 1.5° edge-to-edge (2.5° pitch) and 1.5° centre-to-centre also offered; random layout (Fig 3b) has 10 squares (50 dots at 5 per square) in a region of about 8 × 6° and a minimum centre distance of 1.5° (assumed); clusters are 3 × 5 as in Fig 3a, nearest dots 2° apart, fixation cross at the midpoint (not mentioned in the paper); the occlusion manipulation of 3.1.1 is not offered; Exp 2 off-interval distribution (uniform 1–60 s here), fixation cross at the screen centre over the square, the report prompts, and that responses are held internally only; Exp 3 screen region for random positions (uniform over the canvas; positions within 1° of the cross excluded); Exp 4 column spacing (centre of left pair 1.5° and right pair 2.05° from the square centre, as measured from Fig 5), SOA, and phase (pair left dot together with the square's bottom-left and top-right dots, following the numbering in Fig 5); Exp 5a SOA, direction of rotation (clockwise), equal steps of 30° or 22.5° (both inside the paper's 20–30° range; 22.5° gives exactly 4 steps to 90°); Exp 5b cycles per row (3), lattice pitch (1°), dots lit as one checkerboard half of the lattice per frame, a single A→B switch with replay, no fixation mark; Exp 6 start SOA (500 ms), end SOA (100 ms) and rate of lowering (the paper says "gradually"; 60 s here); in the paper Exp 6 reports were verbal, so no response control is provided. Fig 8 (static dot array) is not an experiment and is omitted.
