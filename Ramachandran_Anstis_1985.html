Ramachandran & Anstis (1985) — Perceptual organization in multistable apparent motion

Two diagonally opposite corners of a 1° "conceptual square" flash, then are replaced by the other two, in a continuous cycle. Select an experiment and view the display as the participants did.

Experimenter parameters

Reconstruction of Ramachandran, V. S. & Anstis, S. M. (1985), Perception 14, 135–143. Only the physical dot flashes are drawn; no percept, axis, path or result is shown or scored. Original: P-4 phosphor CRT, Apple II, viewed at 1 m; here 1° = 64 px (so 1° square = 64 px, 3′ dot ≈ 3.2 px), which equals the paper's geometry only if the page is viewed at the matching distance. Light dots on dark.
ParameterPaper value (page)
Square side / dot diameter / distance1° / 3′ of arc / 1 m (p.136, Method)
CycleTwo diagonal dots on, then switched off and replaced by the other two; repeated continuously (p.136). Only two dots visible at any instant.
SOAHand-held potentiometer, continuous 25–500 ms; SOA rather than ISI varied (p.136). Used 100–200 ms in earlier experiments (p.141); Exp 1 "3–4 frames/s" (p.137); Exp 5b: 200 ms within pairs, 300 ms between row A and row B (p.140); Exp 6: >350 ms to will rotation, <300 ms reverted (p.141).
Exp 1 (p.136–138)Several squares simultaneously, side 1°, distance between squares 1.5°, 3–4 frames/s; regular arrays (Fig 3a) and random positions (Fig 3b); 12 naive students. 3.1.1: two clusters ~2° apart, one rotated exactly 45°, one per hemifield, 3 subjects. 3.1.2: dichoptic (crossed polaroids; upper two rows to one eye, third row to the other, binocular fixation cross between rows 2 and 3).
Exp 2 (p.138)Familiarised with drawings "percept 1/percept 2"; shown square; reports axis (vertical/horizontal); display on 30 s, switched off automatically, reappears after various random intervals; fixation cross on screen; told axis might or might not have changed; 6 naive subjects.
Exp 3 (p.138–139)Square "random walk": after every 3–4 cycles switched off and reappears at a random screen location after about 500 ms; fixation on a central cross; 6 naive subjects. (The head-tilt variant, eyes closed and head rotated 90° between presentations, cannot be done on a page.)
Exp 4 (p.139)Single ambiguous square in the centre, surrounded by array of dots in unambiguous motion (single pairs of dots, Fig 5).
Exp 5a (p.139)3 cycles, then square replaced by slightly rotated one, 3 more cycles, repeated in 20°–30° steps up to 90°; fixation cross in the middle of the square.
Exp 5b (p.140)Square embedded in a rectangular matrix of squares; column A (a few cycles) switched off and replaced by row B; SOA 200 ms within pairs, 300 ms between A and B.
Exp 6 (p.140–141)Same four-dot display; 3 subjects asked to will clockwise/anticlockwise rotation at long SOA while SOA was gradually lowered.
Assumptions / unspecified in the paper: default SOA in Exp 2, 3, 4, 5a, basic (200 ms); ISI taken as 0 (each frame lasts one SOA; paper does not give the ISI); square spacing read as centre-to-centre 1.5° (could be edge-to-edge); number of squares in the random layout (15) and the array size (3 × 5; clusters 3 × 3) and minimum spacing; position of the fixation cross in Exp 1 (clusters: between them; otherwise none); Exp 2 off-interval distribution (uniform 1–60 s here), when the display stays on after reappearing (until a report is made), and the number of trials; Exp 3 screen region for random positions; Exp 4 layout, pair separation (1°), pair timing and phase of the surround pairs (all in phase, same SOA, horizontal or vertical excursions as in the text); Exp 5a SOA, direction of rotation (clockwise here), no gap between steps, restart at 0° after 90°; Exp 5b cycles per row (3), row length and matrix size, no fixation mark; Exp 6 rate of SOA lowering (the paper says "gradually"; 60 s from 500 to 100 ms here). The dichoptic condition (3.1.2) is not reproducible without polarising filters and is not drawn. Fig 8 (static dot array) is not an experiment and is omitted.
