Shiffrar & Freyd (1990) — Apparent motion of the human body

Two pictures alternate (picture 1, blank, picture 2, blank, ...) as in a tachistoscope. Stimulus duration (SD), blank interstimulus interval (ISI) and the stimulus pair were varied. The observer watched for as many cycles as desired and then marked the answer sheet.
Watch the alternating pictures for as many cycles as you like, then indicate the path of motion on the answer sheet (options: A, B, A&B or other). Sometimes the path may be very clear, sometimes there may be several paths, or no motion. Methods (Shiffrar & Freyd, 1990, Psychological Science 1(4), 257–264). Photographs cannot be reproduced here: body pairs are simple drawn figures in square frames (Fig. 1 photos are roughly square) with a plain background; poses are approximations (Fig. 1 shows only two example pairs; the leg pair is cropped below the waist, the arm pair above the thigh). The actual stimuli were full-colour photographs of a model, stationary except for one or two limbs. Clocks were photographs of a real wall clock. On the original answer sheet path A was the shortest path and path B a longer path between the two positions; the diagrams themselves are not reproduced.
