Shiffrar & Freyd (1990) — Apparent motion of the human body

Two photographs (or drawings) alternate in a tachistoscope. Only the stimulus duration (SD), the blank interstimulus interval (ISI) and the stimulus pair were varied. The observer watched for as long as desired and then marked which path(s) of motion, if any, had been experienced.
Watch the alternation for as long as you like, then indicate the path of motion you experienced (answer sheet options: A, B, A&B or other). Sometimes the path may be very clear, sometimes you may see several paths, or no motion at all. (Nothing is recorded or scored here.)

Methods (Shiffrar & Freyd, 1990, Psychological Science 1(4), 257–264). Photographs cannot be reproduced here: the body pairs are simple drawn silhouettes in poses approximating those described (Fig. 1 shows only two example pairs); the actual stimuli were full-colour photographs of a model whose pose was stationary except for one or two limbs. The answer-sheet line drawings of the candidate paths are not reproduced (they would depict paths); only the response options are shown.
