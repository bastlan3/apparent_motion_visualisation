Ramachandran & Anstis (1983) — extrapolation of motion path (“visual momentum”)

Two dot streams move in opposite directions along parallel rows and meet at an ambiguous quartet. Established motion biases the crossing toward streaming (straight-through) over bouncing — even against proximity.
Fixate the centre dot. At the crossing, do the streams pass straight through (streaming) or bounce apart? Press what you see — it nudges the row separation toward the balance point.

Faithful reconstruction of Ramachandran, V. S. & Anstis, S. M. (1983), Vision Research 23, 83–85. Four test dots form an ambiguous motion quartet (two diagonal pairs alternating); it is embedded between two long parallel rows of dots flashed sequentially in opposite directions (only two lit at a time; ISI = 0, SOA set by duration; viewing 1 m, dots ≈ 4′, within-row spacing b ≈ 1°). With the quartet alone, streaming and bouncing are equiprobable near a = b. When the rows are in motion, observers overwhelmingly see straight-line streaming — the row separation a must be cut to ≈ 60% of b before bouncing becomes equiprobable (≈ 80% for merely stationary rows). This “visual momentum” overrides the proximity rule: once motion is established in a direction, it tends to continue. The percept (streaming vs bouncing) is the observer's — only the real flashed dots are drawn. Balance found by a streaming/bouncing staircase.
