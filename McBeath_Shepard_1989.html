McBeath & Shepard (1989) — Window technique

A shape alternates on the two sides of a partition. 180 trials in random order.
Adjust the height of the window (↑ / ↓ keys, or hold the buttons) to obtain the strongest illusion of smooth motion of the object back and forth through it. Try for a placement at or above the straight line between the two positions of the shape. Then rate your confidence in the placement: 3 = high confidence, well-defined path; 1 = low confidence, poorly defined path; 2 = intermediate.

Methods (McBeath & Shepard, 1989, Perception & Psychophysics 46(4), 333–337)

DisplayIBM XT, low-resolution graphics (p. 334). Resolution, luminance, colours, polarity not given. Assumed: light shapes on black, mid-grey wall, anti-aliased canvas.
Viewing distance / size≈ 80 cm; maximum diameter of each shape ≈ 1.5° (p. 334). Rendered at 32 px/° (shape ≈ 48 px). Physical size depends on your monitor; not calibrated.
LayoutVertical partition in the middle; shape alternates left and right of it; a gap (window) in the partition just large enough to admit the rigid passage of the shape (Fig. 1b). Both shapes placed closer to the bottom of the screen. Wall width (10 px), wall height (full field), field size, absence of fixation mark: assumptions/unspecified. Window height = twice the shape's largest radius from its centre, so any orientation fits.
ShapesNarrow (line drawing after Farrell, 1983), Medium (silhouette resembling Farrell & Shepard, 1981 polygons), Wide (Pac Man: circle with pie segment removed) — Fig. 1a. Outlines here are redrawn by eye from Fig. 1a; exact vertices and mouth angle (80° total) unspecified.
OrientationOrientation difference 90° or 180°. Narrow and medium shapes oriented with their axis of elongation through the centre of rotation specified by kinematic geometry for the pair; for 90° trials the centre lies below the line joining the two positions (upward-curving pairs). Pac Man never symmetric about a vertical axis. Pac Man: left mouth at 45° up-right, right = left + difference (assumption). Which of the pair is on the left, and the direction of rotation, unspecified.
SeparationCentre-to-centre 3°, 6°, 9°.
TimingEach shape on for 550 ms; ISI 0, 50, 100, 150, 200 ms (SOA 550–750 ms). Here counted in display frames at the measured refresh rate (…); at 60 Hz: 33 frames = 550 ms, ISI = 0/3/6/9/12 frames = 0/50/100/150/200 ms. Shapes alternate continuously until the rating is given; first frame of a trial shows the left shape immediately (unspecified).
ResponseJoystick adjusts window height; confidence rating 3-point scale after the final placement. Here: arrow keys / buttons at a constant rate (≈ 1.9°/s); allowed range 1.5° below to 7° above the line; start height random in that range (all unspecified).
Design3 shapes × 3 separations × 2 orientation differences × 5 ISIs × 2 replications = 180 trials per observer, random order (≈ 45 min). 10 observers. Observers were not told the experimenters' ideas about path curvature.
