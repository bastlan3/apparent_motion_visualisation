Burt & Sperling (1981) - multiple-path motion stimulus

Display setup
Resize the bar until it matches the width of a credit card (8.56 cm), then set your eye-to-screen distance.

Methods (Psychol. Rev. 88, 171-195)

StimulusOne horizontal row of evenly spaced dots, flashed repeatedly; between presentations the row moves horizontally by H and downward by V; the stack of rows sweeps through a rectangular window (p. 173-174, Fig. 1a). Observers fixate a stationary point (p. 174).
Dot separation D17 mm (1.70 cm) = 29.3 arcmin at 2 m (p. 174)
Row shift H, V (Exp 1)H = +/-6.8 mm (11.7 arcmin), V = 1.8 mm (3.09 arcmin) (p. 174, Table 1)
Interval tExp 1: ten values, logarithmic steps, 12-56 ms (p. 175). Exp 2/3: seven values per configuration (p. 179); the seven values are not listed in the paper - editable list here, default 7 log steps 10-60 ms.
FlashEach row presentation = 2 refreshes of a fast P4 phosphor, 3 ms apart (p. 175). Here: the row is lit for one display frame per presentation and blank for the remaining frames of t; at one frame per t there is no blank gap (sustained). Double refresh and phosphor decay not emulated.
Control stimuli (Exp 1)M1: every other dot removed (dot separation 2D, same H, V, t). M2: every other row removed (H, V, t doubled, separation D). (Table 1)
Exp 1 session40 trials per session = 4 trial kinds (P1 right, P1 left, P2 right, P2 left) x 10 t, pseudorandom order, same t for ambiguous and control (p. 175). Each trial: M1,2 and the control alternated three times, 1 s pause between stimuli; P1 trials begin with M1. Rating 0-6 at the end of the trial: 0 = undetectable; 1 = 20% as strong as the reference; ... 5 = equal strength; 6 = stronger in M1,2 than in the control. P2 trials: the paper does not state which stimulus comes first; control first assumed. Authors were the two observers.
Window and fixationRows within a 22 cm x 6 cm area (6.3 x 1.72 deg at 2 m); bottom of area 4 cm (1.15 deg) above the fixation point (p. 175). Window is not drawn (dark room).
Viewing2 m (Exp 1, 3), 1 m and 2 m (Exp 2); binocular, dark room, display surface 0.058 cd/m2; dot energy about 1.6e-6 cd-s per refresh (p. 175). Here sizes are drawn in visual angle for the paper's distance and converted with your calibration.
Exp 218 configurations, D = 17 mm; H, V pairs of Table 2 (menu), each viewed from 1 m and 2 m (distance scales all angular sizes). One sweep of the row through the window per trial (max 3 s); forced choice right/left (p. 177-181). Block here: 7 t x 8 trials (4 as defined, 4 mirror) = 56 trials, random order; the paper combined three determinations from separate sessions, balanced across transition types and distances (not implemented).
Exp 3As Exp 2 with dots, rings, alternating dots/rings, or alternating orthogonal line elements; D = 17 mm, H = 6.8 mm, V = 1.84, 2.45, 3.06 mm, 2 m (p. 181-184). Block: 2 repetitions x 3 V x 2 reflections x 7 t = 84 trials, random order; the paper ran four element blocks then the reverse order (not implemented).
Elements (Fig. 4)Sizes estimated from the Fig. 4c scale bar (uncertain): dot 1.5 mm diameter, octagonal ring 3.0 mm across, line element 5.9 mm long drawn at +45 and -45 deg alternately (Fig. 4a). The paper gives energies per refresh (dot 1.6e-6, ring 1.9e-6, line 10.3e-6 cd-s); all are drawn at the same white here (not emulated). Exp 1 dots use the same dot size (assumption).
Other assumptionsFixation mark = small dot; 500 ms fixation before each trial; 1 s between trials; sweep starts at the top of the window, horizontally centred; Exp 1 sweep length (to window bottom) is an assumption, the paper does not give it; default screen scale 37.8 px/cm, default viewing distance 200 cm (paper's 2 m). Responses are kept in memory only (CSV download of conditions and responses).
TimingRefresh rate measured at load (median rAF interval over 60 frames); every duration is a whole number of frames at that rate and scheduled by frame count. Realised values are in the line under the stage. A trial in which a frame interval exceeded 1.5 frames is flagged and repeated at the end of the block.
