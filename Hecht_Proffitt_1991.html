Layout of the response interfaceThe response panel in use (probe-on button, goodness, difficulty/depth sliders, grid controls, Next) overlays the lower part of the canvas, so no scrolling is needed (the goodness scale was at the bottom of the screen, p.1094). Domino3.2 x 1.6 x 0.8 cm (Exp 3: 2.6 x 1.3 x 0.65 cm); top marked with dots, ends with lines, sides colour-coded; long axis along the object rayp.1094; p.1099. Top: two halves split by a line with 1 + 2 dots (as in Figs 4/6, not stated in text); ends neutral grey with 2 lines; sides blue/green; counts, sizes and colours assumed (scaled with domino size).
GeometryTwo fixed dominos, centres 13.5 cm apart on screen (Exp 3: 6.75 cm), placed symmetrically about the invisible system axis on object rays from origin O, angle theta to the axis. For small theta, O lies far behind/off the display, so the page puts the midpoint of the two domino centres at screen centre (jittered within the 4-cm circle) and O is implicit; beta1/beta2 are applied in the system frame (B1 about the system axis, B2 perpendicular to it in the picture plane). Internal rotations alpha1 (about x', long axis), alpha2 (about y'); alpha3 = 0. System rotated by beta1 (external x = system axis) or beta2 (y); beta3 = in-plane orientation of the axis.pp.1094–1095, Fig. 4. Rotation sign, rotation order, and applying the same alpha to both dominos are assumptions.
ProjectionParallel (orthographic) in Exps 1–4; polar (perspective) at 25 cm in Exp 5pp.1095–1099, 1101. Exp 5 eye on screen-centre normal; rotation pivot at the midpoint of the pair so depth is symmetric about the screen plane; the 3-D distance is rescaled so the on-screen centre separation is 13.5 cm (the 30° projected orientation difference is not enforced).
3-D theta and distanceB1: tan(theta3D) = tan(theta2D)/cos(beta1), distance = 13.5/cos(beta1). B2: distance 13.5; theta3D from theta2D (relation garbled in the PDF; I used tan(theta3D) = tan(theta2D) cos(beta2), which reproduces the Exp 5 values).Appendix pp.1102–1103; B2 form inferred
Origin O and axis orientationO randomised within a 4-cm circle at screen centre; axis beta3 randomp.1094. 4 cm taken as the diameter; beta3 random within each 90° sector (stratified) in Exp 1, uniform over 360° in Exps 2–5 (not specified)
TimingEach domino 220 ms on, SOA 500 ms, cycle (D1-ISI-D2-ISI) 1000 ms, continuous. Converted to whole frames at the refresh rate measured at load (median of 60 frames) and scheduled by counting frames; realised ms are shown in the display line under the canvas, with a warning when a duration is not a whole number of frames (at 60 Hz: 13 frames = 216.7 ms, SOA 30, cycle 60).p.1094
ProbeTurned on by the observer; flashes in the ISIs for 160 ms (whole frames at the measured rate); starts at a random extreme end of the system axis near the screen border; moved continuously along the axis. Window = two pointed triangles separated enough for the domino to pass; or a third domino at an intermediate position and orientation.p.1094. Probe onset about 50 ms after domino offset; probe range from the screen border (1.5-cm margin) on one side of the pair midpoint to the other; gap 4 cm (3.2 cm in Exp 3); triangle size; probe domino drawn with the system-axis orientation and the stimulus alpha values; slider/keys as control device (Exp 2 ratings used a thumb wheel).
Exp 1 (pp.1094–1095)theta = 30°, beta1 = beta2 = 0; alpha1, alpha2 each 0, 15, 30, 45, 60° (25 trials per block); each trial once with window, once with domino probe (order counterbalanced); random order in block; four practice trials; goodness scale (good–poor) after placement; break after half the trials.p.1094–1095
Exp 2 (pp.1095–1098)15 stimuli: 2D (alpha1 = alpha2 = 20°; theta 30° or 35°), B1 (beta1 34.5, 36.1, 50.9, 62.3, 72.2°; 2D theta 3.1, 7.1, 12.9, 20.6, 25.4°), B2 (beta2 48.8, 60.3, 61.0, 67.8, 72.2°; 2D theta 10, 15, 20, 25, 30°), alpha = 0 for B1/B2. Each shown twice (window block, domino block; 30 trials, break after 15). Probe moves with the system orientation. Two ratings, continuous 0–100 thumb wheel: difficulty (easy–difficult) and depth (in depth–in plane).pp.1095–1098. Which 2D pairs use 30 vs 35 not stated (I used 30, 30, 30, 35, 35). The two ratings are asked in sequence, difficulty first then depth (p.1097); both sliders start at the centre, which the paper does not specify. The 2D theta values are taken as the projected angles the Appendix formula starts from.
Exp 3 (pp.1098–1099)Same 15 stimuli at half size, 64 cm, viewing box, monocular. Each observer saw 15 stimuli in one block; one half of the observers used the window probe and the other half the domino probe (here selectable per block). After placement a key shows a regularly spaced grid drawn in perspective; it can be turned in 5° steps about a vertical or a horizontal axis through its centre (not both). Rating scales dropped.pp.1098–1099. Grid 24 x 24 cm, 3-cm spacing (scaled down proportionally, 8 x 8 cells, when the window is too short to show it whole above the response bar), starts frontoparallel; probe hidden once grid appears; no fixed number of practice trials beyond four.
Exp 4 (pp.1099–1101)Display size as Exp 1–2, viewing as Exp 3. System rotated about x (B1) or y (B2) by 30 or 60°; domino orientation parallel to the object ray, alpha1 = 30, alpha2 = 30, or both 30. Domino probe only; grid task.p.1099–1101. The text lists four orientations but counts 12 stimuli; I present all four (16). theta2D = 30° assumed.
Exp 5 (p.1101)As Exp 4 without box; 25 cm; polar projection; 9 stimuli: B1 beta1 30/45/60 (theta3D 33.7, 39.2, 49.1°), B2 beta2 30/45/60 (theta3D 26.6, 22.2, 16.1°), 2D (theta 16.1, 32.7, 49.1°); alpha1 = alpha2 = 10°.p.1101
