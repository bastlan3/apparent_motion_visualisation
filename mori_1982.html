Mori (1982) — Apparent motion path: serial concatenation of translations & rotations

A line is flashed in two positions/orientations (vertical→horizontal); a dim probe line is then flashed between them. The observer judges the probe to map out the perceived motion path.
Watch the apparent motion of the line, then judge the briefly-flashed probe. Here you set the probe to match the orientation & height of the moving line where the probe appears.

Faithful reconstruction of the stimulus & apparatus in Mori, T. (1982), Biol. Cybern. 44, 31–34. Line segments 1.1 cm = 1.26°; BB′ = 3.0 cm = 3.4°; viewing 50 cm; dark room. Inducing lines bright (5.8 mL), probe dim (2.6 mL). The interpolated rotating/translating line is the observer's percept and is deliberately not drawn — only the discrete flashes (stim 1, stim 2, then one probe) appear, since drawing a connecting path would impose one of the very motion schemes Mori set out to test. The original probe was a 10 ms flash judged CW/CCW with an interleaved staircase; here it is rendered slightly longer and set by adjustment. The "70 ms" ISI is read from Fig. 1b (OCR-ambiguous) and left adjustable. ITI defaults below the paper's 4000 ms for watchability.
