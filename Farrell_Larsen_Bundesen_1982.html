Farrell, Larsen & Bundesen (1982) — apparent rotational movement

Two views of one pattern alternate; the observer adjusts the stimulus-onset asynchrony with three keys. Methods reconstruction: Perception 11, 541–546.
Keys: → right key shorter SOA (×10/11)  ·  ← left key longer SOA (×11/10)  ·  Space / ↓ centre key register a limit.
Press the right key repeatedly until the impression of rigid movement disappears, then register (centre key). Then press the left key repeatedly until the impression of rigid movement reappears, then register again (centre key) — this ends the trial.

The second curved element named in the Fig. 1 caption is not visible in the printed figure and is not drawn here.
Orientation 0 = upright; positive rotation = clockwise; first member of each pair = orientation u (paper gives neither).
On-time of each member = SOA − 50 ms (implied by constant 50 ms ISI), rounded to whole display frames; ISI = 3 frames. The alternation runs until the trial is ended.
No limits on SOA are imposed except that on-time is at least 1 frame. Stroke colour (green, P31-like), line width (2 px) and luminance are uncalibrated; 40 Hz intensification is not modelled. Screen scale defaults to 4 px/mm; set it to the real value of your screen to get true physical size.
Exact instruction wording, practice trials, the blank between trials (here: next trial starts when the participant presses Start/Enter) and the rest-break format are not given; the instruction text above is a paraphrase. The SOA value is not shown to the participant.
Viewing distance cannot be enforced; the 1 m / 0.75 m sizes in degrees hold only if you view from that distance at the calibrated scale.
