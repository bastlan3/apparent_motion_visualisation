Faithful reconstruction of Farrell, J. E., Larsen, A. & Bundesen, C. (1982), Perception 11, 541–546. The prototype figure (a vertical bar with a short top and two curved sector elements — arcs of circles centred at the bar's base) is scaled by 1/2/3/4× and shown in continuous alternation between two orientations differing by a picture-plane rotation about the base of 30/60/90/120°, ISI = 50 ms. The observer speeds up (SOA ×10/11) until rigid rotation disappears (SOA₁) then slows (×11/10) until it reappears (SOA₂); minimum SOA = their mean. Critical SOA rises linearly with angle, and the slope is virtually size-invariant — evidence for an upper bound on the object's angular velocity (≈ 0.68°/ms) rather than the linear velocity of its parts. The translation control alternates two copies displaced horizontally (2/3/4°); its slope gives a linear-velocity bound (≈ 0.9 m/s). Only the two real alternating views are drawn — the rigid rotation is the observer's percept.
