Robins & Shepard (1977) — Spatio-temporal probing of apparent rotational movement

A vertical and a horizontal white bar alternate with coincident centres. A brief yellow dot is shown in the blank interval between them, in one of the two upper quadrants, and the repeating sequence runs continuously.
"You will see a vertical bar and a horizontal bar, alternating in rapid succession. This may give the appearance of a movement through space of a bar, rocking back and forth. Do you see that? Through which quadrants does the bar seem to pass? Now a yellow dot should appear at some point in the bar's path, during the downswing of the top end. Your task is to say whether the dot appears before or after the bar passes the dot's place of appearance."
"... take as long as you need to feel really certain about it. Your response will not be timed. Please do not guess unless and until you have really tried to decide and cannot. ... judge each trial independently."
"There will be two types of blocks of trials. In one case, the dot will appear in a quadrant through which the bar seems to pass; in the other case, the dot will be in a quadrant through which the bar is not passing. In this case you are to make the analogous judgment, that is, whether the probe appears before or after the bar would pass that point if it were rotating in the opposite direction, or if the probe were in the symmetrically opposite position."
(Abridged from the instructions quoted on p. 13. If the direction of rotation changes for you, switch the probe quadrant below, as the experimenter would. Responses are not recorded; no feedback.)

Methods (Robins & Shepard, 1977, Perception & Psychophysics 22(1), 12–18; p. 13).
DisplayIconix 6192 four-channel tachistoscope; white bars and yellow probe on a black background; binocular viewing.
Cycle (760 ms)(1) vertical white bar 200 ms; (2) black field, pre-probe interval ISI₁ = 30–130 ms in 20-ms steps; (3) yellow probe dot 20 ms; (4) black post-probe interval ISI₂ = 30–130 ms, so ISI₁ + probe + ISI₂ = 180 ms (hence ISI₂ = 160 − ISI₁); (5) horizontal white bar 200 ms; (6) black field 180 ms; then repeat from (1).
Bars8.9 × 1.3 cm each; viewing distance about 90 cm; length subtends 5°40′. Centres coincide at display centre, marked by a small black dot.
Probe0.6-cm yellow dot (25′), in one of the two upper quadrants at 30°, 40°, 50° or 60° from vertical on a circular arc of 3.2 cm radius from the pivot centre.
TaskSay whether the dot appears before or after the bar passes the dot's position (response not timed; no feedback). Experimental condition: probe in the quadrant where the observer sees the rotation; control: probe in the other quadrant (judge as if the bar rotated the other way / probe at the symmetric position).
Trials12 subjects; per condition and probe angle one block of 12 trials (each of 6 delays twice, random order); 6 practice trials per condition per session; two 1-h sessions. Blocks were run with the dot position fixed within block.
FixationNot instructed. Fixation point not used (black centre dot was only a pivot marker).
Assumptions / unspecified in the paper: luminance of bars and probe, and background luminance, are not given (white-on-black rendered at full screen contrast; yellow #ffdc00). Screen scale is arbitrary (1 cm drawn as 18 px, so proportions match but visual angle depends on your screen and distance; the paper's 90 cm gives 5°40′ only if the bar is about 8.9 cm on screen). Dot is a hard-edged circle; the size of the black pivot dot is not given (5 px here). Whether the probe appeared in every repeated cycle is not stated; here it is shown in every cycle. Response was spoken to the experimenter; the buttons are a substitute. Which quadrant the observer perceives rotation in (experimental vs control) was determined per observer and is not manipulated by the stimulus, so the page has no condition switch: choose the probe quadrant and judge whichever one you see the rotation through. The page shows no path, answer or data. Timing is counted in display frames assuming 60 Hz (16.7 ms): the realised durations are listed under the controls (the 20-ms probe becomes 1 frame = 16.7 ms; the 760-ms cycle becomes 46 frames = 767 ms). On a display with another refresh rate the durations scale accordingly. Practice trials (6 per condition) are not implemented. The cycle keeps running while you respond, as in the paper; a new delay takes effect at the next cycle start.
