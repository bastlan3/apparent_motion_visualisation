Robins & Shepard (1977) — Spatio-temporal probing of apparent rotational movement

A vertical and a horizontal white bar alternate with coincident centres. A brief yellow dot is shown in the blank interval between them, in one of the two upper quadrants, and the repeating sequence runs continuously.
"This experiment is concerned with apparent movement. I will display a vertical bar and a horizontal bar, alternating in rapid succession. This may give the appearance of a movement through space of a bar, rocking back and forth. Do you see that? Through which quadrants does the bar seem to pass? Now a yellow dot should appear at some point in the bar's path, during the downswing of the top end. Your task is to say whether the dot appears before or after the bar passes the dot's place of appearance. If at any time the direction of rotation should change for you, please let me know and the dot position will be changed accordingly. You may find the judgment rather difficult and you should take as long as you need to feel really certain about it. Your response will not be timed. Please do not guess unless and until you have really tried to decide and cannot. Do not expect there always to be an equal number of 'before' and 'after' responses for any given dot position, indeed do not make any assumptions about their relative frequencies but judge each trial independently. After each block of 12 trials you may rest while I change the position of the dot. There will be two types of blocks of trials. In one case, the dot will appear in a quadrant through which the bar seems to pass, as you have just seen; in the other case, the dot will be in a quadrant through which the bar is not passing-like this. In this case you are to make the analogous judgment, that is, whether the probe appears before or after the bar would pass that point if it were rotating in the opposite direction, or if the probe were in the symmetrically opposite position."
Quadrants are numbered as in Fig. 1B: 1 upper right, 2 upper left, 3 lower left, 4 lower right.
Through which quadrants does the bar seem to pass?
Display during the report: As on p. 13, first view the alternation without the dot and report the quadrants; then switch the probe on. Starting practice or a block switches it on.

Methods (Robins & Shepard, 1977, Perception & Psychophysics 22(1), 12–18; p. 13).
DisplayIconix 6192 four-channel tachistoscope; white bars and yellow probe on a black background; binocular viewing.
Cycle (760 ms)(1) vertical white bar 200 ms; (2) black field, pre-probe interval ISI₁ = 30–130 ms in 20-ms steps; (3) yellow probe dot 20 ms; (4) black post-probe interval ISI₂ = 30–130 ms, so ISI₁ + probe + ISI₂ = 180 ms (hence ISI₂ = 160 − ISI₁); (5) horizontal white bar 200 ms; (6) black field 180 ms; then repeat from (1).
Bars8.9 × 1.3 cm each; viewing distance about 90 cm; length subtends 5°40′. Centres coincide at display centre, marked by a small black dot.
Probe0.6-cm yellow dot (25′ as stated in the paper; 0.6 cm at 90 cm actually subtends about 23′, a small internal inconsistency in the paper, so the page uses 0.6 cm and prints the computed angle), in one of the two upper quadrants at 30°, 40°, 50° or 60° from vertical on a circular arc of 3.2 cm radius from the pivot centre.
TaskSay whether the dot appears before or after the bar passes the dot's position (response not timed; no feedback). Experimental condition: probe in the quadrant where the observer sees the rotation; control: probe in the other quadrant (judge as if the bar rotated the other way / probe at the symmetric position).
Trials (p. 13–14)12 subjects; per condition (probe in perceived quadrant / other quadrant) 4 blocks of 12 trials, one block per probe angle (30, 40, 50, 60°); within a block each of the 6 delays twice, order random and independent per block. Each session = 4 blocks, block order randomised with the 8 subconditions counterbalanced over order positions (not implemented; the experimenter chooses angle and quadrant). 6 practice trials in each of the two conditions (12 in total) at session start, conditions and trials randomised, no feedback (p. 14). Dot position fixed within a block; subjects rested while the experimenter changed it. Two 1-h sessions per subject.
FixationNot instructed. Fixation point not used (black centre dot was only a pivot marker).
Assumptions / unspecified in the paper: luminance of bars and probe and of the background are not given (white-on-black at full screen contrast; yellow #ffdc00). Sizes are drawn in cm at the paper's 90 cm distance and rescaled for your viewing distance and calibrated pixels per cm, so 8.9 cm at 90 cm is 5°40′ only if the distance setting equals 90 cm. Dot is a hard-edged circle; size of the black pivot dot not given (0.2 cm assumed). Number of cycles viewed per trial, whether the probe appears in every cycle once introduced, and how trial boundaries were marked are not stated; here, after the bars-only demonstration used for the quadrant report (p. 13), the probe appears in every cycle and the cycle runs continuously. The page opens in the bars-only state; free mode needs the probe switched on by the experimenter. Response was spoken; buttons are a substitute and are stored internally only. The observer reports the quadrant pair (1 and 3, or 2 and 4); the probe side is derived from the block type (perceived upper quadrant for the experimental type, the other upper quadrant for the control type). The "rotation changed for me" button flips the reported pair, which moves the probe, and is logged as a flag. In block mode the block type is fixed; in practice mode it varies from trial to trial (shuffled). In block and practice modes the delay, angle and quadrant buttons are locked while a trial sequence runs; the next trial's delay and probe side take effect together at the next cycle start after a response, and responses are ignored until then; the logged delay is the trial's own.
Timing: the refresh rate is measured at load (median of about 60 rAF intervals); every duration is converted to whole frames at that rate and the cycle is scheduled by counting frames. Rounding rule: bars 200 ms and final blank 180 ms are rounded independently; the probe is exactly the rounded 20 ms (at least 1 frame) at every delay; ISI₁ is rounded independently; ISI₂ is the remainder so that ISI₁ + probe + ISI₂ equals the rounded 180 ms. If a very low refresh rate makes ISI₁ + probe + ISI₂ exceed the rounded 180 ms, ISI₁ is capped so the sum stays equal to it and a warning is shown. The realised durations are printed in the display line. A warning is shown if a paper duration is not a whole number of frames at the measured rate.
