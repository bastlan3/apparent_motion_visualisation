Robins & Shepard (1977) — Spatio-temporal probing of apparent rotational movement

A vertical and a horizontal white bar alternate with coincident centres (an ambiguous 90° rotation). A brief yellow probe dot is interjected in the blank interval between them; the observer judges whether the dot appeared before or after the bar "passed through" the dot's position.
Watch the repeating sequence. Does the yellow dot appear before or after the bar passes the dot's place of appearance? Take as long as you need; the response is not timed. No feedback is given. (Responses are not recorded here; a response only draws a new random delay.)

Methods (Robins & Shepard, 1977, Perception & Psychophysics 22(1), 12–18; p. 13).
DisplayIconix 6192 four-channel tachistoscope; white bars and yellow probe on a black background; binocular viewing.
Cycle (760 ms)(1) vertical white bar 200 ms; (2) black field, pre-probe interval ISI₁ = 30–130 ms in 20-ms steps; (3) yellow probe dot 20 ms; (4) black post-probe interval ISI₂ = 30–130 ms, so ISI₁ + probe + ISI₂ = 180 ms (hence ISI₂ = 160 − ISI₁); (5) horizontal white bar 200 ms; (6) black field 180 ms; then repeat from (1).
Bars8.9 × 1.3 cm each; viewing distance about 90 cm; length subtends 5°40′. Centres coincide at display centre, marked by a small black dot.
Probe0.6-cm yellow dot (25′), in one of the two upper quadrants at 30°, 40°, 50° or 60° from vertical on a circular arc of 3.2 cm radius from the pivot centre.
TaskSay whether the dot appears before or after the bar passes the dot's position (response not timed; no feedback). Experimental condition: probe in the quadrant where the observer sees the rotation; control: probe in the other quadrant (judge as if the bar rotated the other way / probe at the symmetric position).
Trials12 subjects; per condition and probe angle one block of 12 trials (each of 6 delays twice, random order); 6 practice trials per condition per session; two 1-h sessions. Blocks were run with the dot position fixed within block.
FixationNot instructed. Fixation point not used (black centre dot was only a pivot marker).
Assumptions / unspecified in the paper: luminance of bars and probe, and background luminance, are not given (white-on-black rendered at full screen contrast; yellow #ffdc00). Screen scale is arbitrary (1 cm drawn as 18 px, so proportions match but visual angle depends on your screen and distance; the paper's 90 cm gives 5°40′ only if the bar is about 8.9 cm on screen). Dot is drawn at a hard-edged circle. Whether the probe appeared in every repeated cycle is not stated; here it is shown in every cycle. Response was spoken to the experimenter; the buttons are a substitute. Which quadrant the observer perceives rotation in (experimental vs control) was determined per observer and is not manipulated by the stimulus, so the page has no condition switch: choose the probe quadrant and judge whichever one you see the rotation through. The page shows no path, answer or data. Timing is quantized to display frames (typically 16.7 ms), unlike the tachistoscope.
