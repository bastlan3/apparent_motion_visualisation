Bundesen, Larsen & Farrell (1983) — apparent transformations of size and orientation

Two views of the same character, differing in size and in rotation in the picture plane, alternate continuously with no blank interval. The observer speeds up the alternation until the impression of one shape-preserving moving object breaks down, then slows it again until it returns.
Free viewing, binocular. The pair alternates from an initial SOA of 1000 ms.
Right key (→): faster (SOA decreased by 1/11), repeat until form-preserving movement disappears (plastic deformation, partial movement, flicker); then press centre key (Space).
Left key (←): slower (SOA increased by 1/10), repeat until form-preserving movement reappears; then press centre key (Space), which ends the trial.
SOA 1000 ms

Response (p. 553)right key: SOA decreased by 1/11 per tap until form-preserving movement disappears; centre key registers SOA₁. Left key: SOA increased by 1/10 per tap until it reappears; centre key registers SOA₂ and ends the trial. About 25 practice trials; 4–6 sessions of experimental trials.
Assumptions / not specified in the paper: character typeface and stroke weight (a bold sans-serif is used); exact aspect of the middle format (taken as 2 × the smallest, about 2 × 1.3 cm, with the 7.9 cm side as height); display colour and the 40 Hz raster structure (a plain light-on-dark rendering is used, phosphor luminance not calibrated for your screen); the scale here is 1 cm = 50 px at the canvas's nominal size, so true visual angle depends on your screen and distance (at 60 cm the largest format would subtend about 7.5° × 5.1°); which view comes first within the alternation; key mapping (arrow keys and Space are this page's choice); timing is tied to the browser's frame rate, so very short SOAs are quantised to display frames. The page records and shows no thresholds.
