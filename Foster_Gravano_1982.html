Foster & Gravano (1982) — Overshoot of curvature in visual apparent motion

A curved line and a straight line are flashed in sequence on the left; the observer dials the continuously-present line on the right to match the perceived curvature of the left target line, then rates the motion.
Watch the left-side sequence. On the right, turn the control until that line looks identical to the lower left-side line. Then rate the apparent motion: 0 none · 1 some · 2 smooth (beta).

Faithful reconstruction of the stimulus/apparatus in Foster, D. H. & Gravano, S. (1982), Perception & Psychophysics, 31(5), 411–420. X–Y oscilloscope (P31), binocular viewing at 1.7 m through a view tunnel, uniform white background; lines 2.0° wide, flashed 50 ms. Left = sequentially flashed stimuli; right = a continuously-present line whose curvature (Exp 1–2) or vertical position (Exp 3) the observer adjusts with a rotary control to match the perceived target. Positive SOA = curved line precedes straight line. Curvature is the tangent–chord angle α (sagitta = (L/2)·tan(α/2)); inducing α = 45°. Exp 2 adds a straight line tangential to the curved line, flashed in synchrony with the lower straight line, to suppress the curved→lower motion. Exp 3 uses two 0.125° segments (upper first, SOA 50 ms), separation 0.4–0.8°, matching apparent vertical position. The overshoot itself is the percept and is deliberately not drawn on the left — only the real flashes appear; the right adjustable line is the observer's report. Presentation auto-loops for viewing (the paper showed 3 reps at 2 s, subject-triggered).
